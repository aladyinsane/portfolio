## Problem

Rural health stakeholders had no integrated view of healthcare capacity: provider
availability, specialty shortages, utilization patterns, and population health
needs were scattered across separate datasets that couldn't support statewide
planning decisions on their own.

## Approach

Designed a multi-source analytics platform combining Medicaid claims, Medicare
claims, commercial claims, provider enrollment data, census information,
workforce indicators, and social determinants of health measures. Built reusable
SAS and Python pipelines to classify diagnoses, map conditions to services,
generate provider access measures, and produce Power BI-ready datasets, then
layered interactive visualizations on top for geographic analysis, specialty-level
access evaluation, workforce planning, and provider participation analysis.

## Outcome

Brought multiple statewide claims, provider, demographic, and workforce datasets
into a single analytical environment supporting provider access evaluation and
workforce planning. The reusable pipelines turned what would otherwise have been
a one-time study into a repeatable analytics capability, adaptable for future
refreshes or expansion to additional states, and enabled detailed evaluation of
provider supply, specialty shortages, payer participation, and rural access
disparities statewide.
