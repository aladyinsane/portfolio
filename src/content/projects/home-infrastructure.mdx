## What it is

Two related, ongoing projects, both solving problems for a household of one instead
of a client:

**A self-hosted home automation stack.** A Docker Compose stack running Mosquitto
(MQTT broker), ring-mqtt (bridges a Ring doorbell camera onto MQTT), and Home
Assistant, reachable remotely and securely through Tailscale instead of anything
exposed to the open internet.

**A bill-pay automation system.** A Python system that handles recurring bill logic
against an Excel-based ledger, so bill tracking is automated and auditable instead of
manual and scattered across statements.

## Approach

Same engineering instincts as the paid work, applied without anyone requiring it:
containerized services instead of ad hoc installs, a private mesh network instead of
open ports, and a version-trackable ledger instead of a shoebox of statements. It's
deliberately small in scope right now — one device bridged in cleanly beats five
bridged in sloppily.

## Why it's here

Employers only see the systems thinking you were assigned to apply. This is evidence
it's not switched on and off with a badge — it's how I default to building things,
including the things nobody's paying me to build well.
